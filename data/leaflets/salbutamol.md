# Salbutamol pressurised inhaler: patient information (SYNTHETIC)

> Synthetic leaflet written for the MedSaarthi AI course project from general public knowledge. It is not an official manufacturer leaflet and must not be used for real medical decisions.

## 1. What this medicine is and what it is used for
Salbutamol is a reliever inhaler. It opens the airways quickly in asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and relieves wheezing, chest tightness, and breathlessness.

## 2. What to know before you take it
Tell your doctor if you have heart disease, an overactive thyroid, high blood pressure, or diabetes. If you need your reliever more often than usual, your condition may be getting worse; see your doctor.

## 3. How to use this medicine
Always use this medicine exactly as your doctor has told you. Shake the inhaler. Breathe out gently, put the mouthpiece in your mouth and close your lips around it. Press the canister while breathing in slowly and deeply. Hold your breath for as long as is comfortable, then breathe out slowly. If you need more than one puff, wait about a minute before the next one. A spacer device can make the inhaler easier to use. Use it when you are breathless, as prescribed, not on a fixed timetable unless your doctor says so.

Get urgent medical help if the inhaler does not relieve your breathing as usual.

## 4. Possible side effects
Common: shaking, headache, fast heartbeat. Uncommon: muscle cramps, mouth or throat irritation. Rarely, breathing can get worse straight after use; stop and seek help.

## 5. Other medicines
Tell your doctor if you take beta-blockers (including eye drops), water tablets, or theophylline.

## 6. How to store this medicine
Store below 30 degrees. Do not puncture or burn the canister, even when empty. Keep out of the sight and reach of children.
